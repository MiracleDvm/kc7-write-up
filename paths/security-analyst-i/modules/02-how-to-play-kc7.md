# Module 2: How to Play KC7

> KC7 Security Analyst I path · Difficulty: Beginner · Path completed: October 2026 · Reviewed on: 2026-10-19

## About this module

Unlike the other modules in this path, *How to Play KC7* has no attached dataset and no KQL to write.
It's four short reading checkpoints that explain what KC7 is actually trying to teach, and how to get
the most out of it before diving into real investigations.

## Key points

- **KQL is the tool, not the goal.** The queries are "a flashlight in a dark room": what actually
  matters is knowing where to point it. Syntax can be looked up at any time; investigative intuition
  can't, so that's what the practice is really building.
- **Every scenario hides a complete, self-consistent attack in the data** (reconnaissance, a
  phishing email, a click, a compromise, data theft), and the module's questions only guide attention
  toward it. The job is to actively dig the story out of logs, not to have it handed over.
- **There's no shortcut to investigative skill.** It's built through repetition, pattern recognition,
  and making (and correcting) mistakes across many different cases, not through reading or watching
  alone.
- **The variety is deliberate.** KC7 is built as a long series of different companies, attackers, and
  puzzles (short/long, playful/serious) specifically so the underlying skills get enough repetition to
  become second nature.

## How this shapes my approach to the rest of the path

Going into the next modules, the takeaway is to treat each question as a *lead* to chase rather than a
checklist item to clear, and to document the reasoning that connects one piece of evidence to the
next, since that reasoning is the actual skill being practiced here, not the final answer.
